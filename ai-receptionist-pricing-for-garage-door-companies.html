Which calls belong in your paid minute allowance?

The next choice is coverage scope. Don't forward every call merely because the service can answer around the clock.

Look at how your technicians and office staff divide the day. During installations, someone may answer normal inquiries. After closing, calls may go unanswered.

Separate your calls by purpose

A homeowner asking whether you serve their ZIP code may need a simple answer.

An opener fault may need a technician to review symptoms. A new garage door often needs measurements before a firm estimate.

Should a caller get a booking or an estimate callback?

Direct booking sounds useful, but not every garage-door inquiry deserves an immediate calendar slot.

A replacement-door inquiry

A homeowner asks for a new insulated door. Before quoting, the business may need measurements, finish preferences, and track details.

A measurement visit could help. Booking final installation before checking materials would be premature.

A repair request with an unclear fault

A customer says the opener runs, but the door won't move. The problem might involve the opener or spring hardware.

The receptionist can collect the symptom. A technician makes the repair decision, including the time needed.

An existing customer changing a visit

Someone wants to move a booked installation because garage access has changed. This isn't another new estimate.

The office must identify the existing order before adjusting the visit. A duplicate booking could confuse the crew.

The AutomateNexus Voice Call Catcher plan includes Google Calendar booking and warm transfer. Those functions don't replace job-approval rules.

Where clerical rework affects value

Missing door measurements can require extra calls. A wrong visit type can disrupt the crew. These are potential business costs, not observed product failures.

What are the privacy limits and failure recovery steps?

Phone answering involves customer details, uncertain requests, and safety risks. The business must plan for problems.

Customer records and recording

AutomateNexus Voice includes recordings and transcripts on Call Catcher.

Check record access, caller notices, and retention terms before using live customer data. Avoid sensitive payment details in routine repair notes.

Dangerous door conditions

A caller may report a broken spring, a fallen door, or someone trapped near moving hardware.

Door & Access Systems Manufacturers Association guidance identifies serious risks involving garage-door springs and cables.

The receptionist should gather facts and follow the business's urgent-call policy. It shouldn't guide callers through loaded-spring adjustments.

A trained technician must make the repair decision.

Missed transfers and unavailable staff

A warm transfer can't guarantee a technician will answer. If nobody is available, assign a callback owner.

For an unclear address, call back before dispatch. When the calendar is down, mark the appointment pending.

If service is interrupted, use backup answering and check missed inquiries after recovery.

Recovery rule: Give every unresolved call a named human owner. Don't treat a saved recording as a completed repair request.
