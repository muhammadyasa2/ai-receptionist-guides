Assign an answer to each kind of inquiry

The busiest person isn't always the right person to return every inquiry. A garage door company can sort messages by what each caller wants, then assign the reply.

A homeowner comparing new door styles needs an estimate contact. Someone asking for a copy of an invoice needs the person handling billing. A stranded driver might need urgent attention from the duty technician. All three could arrive while the owner is on a ladder.

The Get Assistant iPhone app combines a business number, AI receptionist, calling, texting, and contact history. The owner doesn't have to run a separate browser dashboard to read new requests.

Use the phone inbox as a list of decisions, not a list of interruptions. The first pass asks who must respond. The second checks what must happen before that person can reply.

For a replacement-door quote, request the property address and a callback number. Staff can then ask about dimensions during a proper sales discussion. Don't let an early estimate turn into a fixed price based on rough details.

A warranty question needs different treatment. Check the work order before making a promise.

Daily ownership rule: Give every open inquiry one named reviewer and a next action. The owner may still approve all field visits. That doesn't mean every message must interrupt an active repair.

Keep estimates apart from promised arrivals

There's a costly difference between asking for a time and agreeing to send a technician. A new customer may request the next morning, but the crew could be fitting a door elsewhere.

Get Assistant's receptionist captures appointment requests and passes them into the iPhone app. Staff confirm those requests. It doesn't place a confirmed appointment directly on a calendar.

That makes the approval step part of the shop's service policy. The person reading a request must check the travel distance, type of job, and crew availability. Only then should anyone send a confirmed arrival window.

Consider a caller who needs a new double-width door measured. The estimator might be free on Friday but unable to install anything that day. Keep the measure and the later installation as separate commitments.

Another caller says an opener hums but the door won't rise. That description isn't enough to pick parts. A technician should assess the fault before a price is final.

An after-hours lockout-style call brings more pressure. The caller may have a vehicle trapped in the garage. Record the stated risk and location for a trained person. Don't promise an emergency visit without accepted duty coverage.

When a requested slot won't work, send a new option. Avoid leaving the caller thinking their first choice was approved.

Decide when a live handoff becomes necessary

An owner can't assess a dangerous door while lifting another one. That's why live handoff needs a clear purpose and a reachable person.

Get Assistant's Starter tier has zero warm-transfer destinations. If immediate human handoff matters, choose Professional with its one destination. The transfer target might be an on-call owner or dispatcher.

Don't use a transfer for every price question. A customer asking about decorative windows can receive a return call after a technician finishes safely. A door that has fallen from its track deserves a different response.

The employee taking that urgent conversation should hear the site address first. Next comes the reported door position and any risk to people. They can then decide the safest next step without guessing at a fix.

Plan for the time no one answers. A warm-transfer feature can't replace an agreed duty roster. Name the backup contact and set a review time for urgent messages.

Before selecting Professional, ask whether the transfer will reach the right person at the hours advertised.

Transfer rule: Pay for Professional when that specific warm-transfer destination changes how your firm responds. Otherwise, an owner-managed callback list may be enough.

Use the last stop to plan tomorrow's work

The value of a late inquiry depends on what happens next morning. A job close to the storage yard may be easy to assess. One beyond your service area needs a deliberate decision.

Review the next day's route before returning new requests. Group inquiries by neighborhood, then by required trade skill. Don't label a measure visit as a repair just because both take place at a garage.

A homeowner may want only a photo-based estimate. Another may require an on-site door measurement. Both should have a clear next step, but neither is a guaranteed booking.

The Get Assistant receptionist collects appointment requests for staff approval. It doesn't write them directly into a calendar. Use that human checkpoint to match the work type with the right time.

Check whether the customer can provide access. A gated property with no entry plan can derail an otherwise open slot. Get the right contact before the van leaves.

Commercial loading-bay doors add another concern. A site may require a manager to approve access. Record the building and contact rather than treating a company name as a complete service address.

When two requests arrive from one neighborhood, do not assume one technician can handle both. A bent track and a new opener estimate involve different parts and time. The owner should accept each visit on its merits.

Software compatibility and dispatch ownership

Some firms schedule visits on paper or a shared calendar. Others manage jobs inside field-service software. Each choice needs a clear home for the next task.

Housecall Pro and Jobber are common examples for a trade business. A direct Get Assistant connection to either isn't verified by the published material here. Ask support to show an actual Jobber or Housecall Pro handoff before relying on it. Check the address, job type, destination, and person who accepts the record.

Without that proven link, the owner can enter approved visits by hand. Make the callback note detailed enough to avoid repeating the whole conversation.

For every visit, decide which sheet or calendar is final. That is the one the crew uses before driving. If the owner changes a time, send the same change to the person assigned the van.

A call-heavy contractor can outgrow the Starter allowance. AutomateNexus Voice Call Catcher offers 500 included minutes at $49. Its entry-plan warm transfer may also suit firms with a staffed dispatcher.

A business with a retail parts counter has a different coverage problem. The employee there may need to check stock, issue receipts, and hand over goods. Keep that counter staffed during pickup hours. Use answering cover for inquiries that arrive while the team is away from the desk.

Caller privacy, safety limits, and failed handoffs

Garage door contractors may get requests from homeowners who aren't on site. One caller might leave access notes for a vacant property. Those details shouldn't travel across an open crew group chat. Keep them with the assigned job and share only what the visiting technician needs.

Get Assistant says its receptionist calls can be transcribed and recording settings are controlled in the app. The vendor states a 30-day retention period for call recordings. Save necessary business notes under your own permitted rules.

Safety needs a firm boundary. DASMA warns that broken springs and damaged cables can cause injury. High-tension spring work belongs with trained technicians. The assistant shouldn't coach a caller through those repairs.

An unresolved inquiry may need attention even when the app is unavailable. Give the duty employee a fallback way to receive urgent messages. Where recording rules apply, check caller notices before storing audio. Don't confirm a slot if no one has approved it.

Failure rule: No urgent job is accepted just because a message exists. The owner or dispatcher must review the hazard, crew capacity, and next step.

Questions owners ask before signing up

Can I use Get Assistant with our job calendar?

Staff must approve appointment requests rather than rely on direct calendar booking. Keep a daily handoff from the app to the crew calendar. Use a human review before dispatch.

Can I try it without paying for a full month?

Yes. Get Assistant offers a seven-day trial and requires a card. Use the trial to check call details, missed transfers, and your actual receptionist minutes.
